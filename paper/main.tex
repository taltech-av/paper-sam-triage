\begin{abstract}
Dense pixel-level annotations remain a major bottleneck in training multi-modal perception models for autonomous driving. 
While the Segment Anything Model (SAM) can generate large volumes of pseudo-labels, its masks frequently drift at boundaries, miss small objects, or introduce spurious segments. 
We propose a multi-agent refinement framework that combines visual–language reasoning with a domain-specific Swin Transformer to validate SAM masks, suppress hallucinations, and recover objects that SAM fails to detect. 
The system uses class-aware agreement thresholds to handle the low mIoU of small traffic participants; a high-confidence bypass threshold is defined and its accuracy–efficiency trade-off studied offline through ablation.
A complementary discovery module identifies regions where Swin predicts objects over SAM background and confirms them with a VLM, enabling automatic recovery of missed instances. 
The refined and augmented masks are used to supervise a CLFTv2 camera–LiDAR fusion model, allowing us to study how improved pseudo-label quality affects downstream segmentation. 
This work demonstrates that combining structured multi-agent reasoning with dataset-adapted quality estimation provides a practical path toward scalable annotation refinement without additional manual labeling.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

High-quality pixel-level annotations remain a major bottleneck for training reliable perception models in autonomous driving. 
Modern camera--LiDAR datasets contain millions of frames, yet only a small fraction can be densely labeled because each mask must be checked for geometric plausibility, occlusion handling, and cross-sensor consistency. 
Foundation models such as the Segment Anything Model (SAM) \cite{kirillov2023sam} reduce the cost of generating pseudo-labels, but their masks often drift at boundaries, hallucinate objects, or miss small traffic participants, limiting their usefulness without additional validation.

Classical segmentation models \cite{long2015fcn,ronneberger2015unet,zhao2017psp,chen2018deeplab} and multi-modal fusion approaches \cite{valada2019selfsupervised,jaritz2019multimodal} still depend on accurate labels, while semi-automatic pipelines based on geometric priors or thresholding \cite{zou2019confidence,lee2013pseudo,jaritz2020xmuda} struggle with subtle errors and provide limited interpretability. 
Recent visual–language models (VLMs) \cite{liu2023llava,alayrac2022flamingo,wang2023voyager,wu2023llmjudge} offer richer semantic priors and structured reasoning, suggesting a more flexible alternative to hand-crafted filters.

We propose a multi-agent annotation system that combines visual–language reasoning with a domain-specific Swin Transformer \cite{liu2021swin} to validate and refine SAM-generated masks. 
The system decomposes mask assessment into specialized agents for object verification, quality estimation, error characterization, and correction, complemented by a deterministic LiDAR consistency check. 
Because destructive decisions amplify false positives in pseudo-labeling pipelines, the triage logic is risk-aware: a mask is removed only when multiple independent signals agree. 
Object-scale multimodal crops ensure reliable VLM judgments, especially for small traffic participants.

Beyond filtering erroneous masks, the system introduces a Swin-based discovery module that identifies objects missed by SAM and confirms them with a VLM, enabling recovery of false negatives. 
The refined and augmented labels supervise CLFTv2 (in preparation, extending~\cite{gu2024clft}), a camera–LiDAR fusion model, allowing us to study how improved pseudo-label quality influences downstream segmentation.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a risk-aware multi-agent framework for validating SAM masks using structured multimodal metadata,
    \item a domain-specific Swin quality judge with class-aware thresholds for small-object reliability,
    \item a VLM-assisted discovery mechanism for recovering missed objects,
    \item and an evaluation on ZOD \cite{alibeigi2023zod} measuring annotation quality and downstream segmentation effects.
\end{itemize}





\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Semantic Segmentation and Annotation in Autonomous Driving}

Semantic segmentation is central to autonomous-driving perception, providing dense scene understanding for planning and safety-critical decision-making. 
Classical camera-based models such as FCN, U-Net, PSPNet, and DeepLab \cite{long2015fcn,ronneberger2015unet,zhao2017psp,chen2018deeplab} established strong baselines, while later work incorporated LiDAR through early-, late-, and feature-level fusion \cite{valada2019selfsupervised,jaritz2019multimodal}. 
Despite these advances, large-scale datasets still require extensive pixel-level annotation, which remains slow and costly.

To reduce labeling effort, prior work explored weak supervision, pseudo-labeling, and geometric priors \cite{zou2019confidence,lee2013pseudo,jaritz2020xmuda}. 
Projection-based consistency checks further leverage LiDAR geometry to validate or refine image-space labels \cite{behley2019semantickitti,caesar2020nuscenes}. 
These approaches improve efficiency but rely on fixed heuristics that struggle with subtle boundary errors, small-object failures, and long-tail classes common in driving scenes.

\subsection{Vision–Language Models and Multi-Agent Reasoning}

Recent vision–language models (VLMs) combine image encoders with language-based reasoning, enabling structured interpretation of visual scenes \cite{liu2024llava,ye2023mplug,wang2024qwen2vl}. 
VLMs have been used to assess label quality, detect inconsistencies, and guide refinement steps \cite{wu2023llmjudge,li2023llmdata}, acting not as segmentation models but as semantic judges over existing predictions.

Multi-agent LLM frameworks extend this idea by decomposing complex tasks into specialized agents that collaborate through structured messages \cite{du2023improving,wang2023voyager}. 
Such systems have been applied to planning, tool use, and quality assessment, demonstrating improved transparency and robustness over monolithic decision modules. 
However, their use in autonomous-driving annotation remains limited, and prior work has not explored multi-agent reasoning over camera--LiDAR metadata for validating and refining SAM-generated masks.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

The system refines SAM-generated masks using a set of specialized visual–LLM agents and a domain-specific segmentation model. 
Each agent receives a multimodal input bundle consisting of an RGB crop, a mask overlay, a LiDAR depth projection, and structured metadata, and returns a categorical decision. 
All outputs feed into a deterministic triage rule designed for safety‑critical annotation workflows.

\subsection{Multimodal Input Construction}

For each SAM proposal, we extract a tight crop around the bounding box and generate three aligned channels: the RGB crop, the mask overlay, and a projected LiDAR depth map. 
Metadata describing geometric properties and LiDAR support is provided as structured text. 
Object‑scale cropping is essential: small traffic participants often occupy only 10–30 pixels, and full‑frame VLM queries fail to resolve them reliably. 
We therefore upscale each crop to at least 224 pixels and query one mask at a time, using the full frame only as optional context.

\subsection{Visual–LLM Agents}

The system decomposes mask assessment into three VLM agents (BBox, Failure‑Mode, and Correction), one Swin‑based quality agent, and one deterministic geometric check.

\paragraph{Bounding‑Box Agent}
Performs blind forced‑choice classification over a fixed vocabulary of object classes and distractors. 
The agent is not told the expected class to avoid agreement bias. 
Outputs are: \texttt{valid} (matches expected class), \texttt{background} (vegetation, building, sky, snow), or \texttt{invalid}. 
The agent sees only the crop; providing the full frame causes scene‑level bias.

\paragraph{Quality Agent}
Evaluates mask correctness using a Swin Transformer \cite{liu2021swin} trained on clean ZOD frames. 
A single forward pass produces a per‑pixel class map for the entire frame. 
Each mask receives an agreement ratio $\alpha$, the fraction of pixels matching the expected class. 
Because Swin achieves high mIoU on large objects but low mIoU on cyclists and pedestrians, we use class‑specific thresholds: $\tau_q^{\text{large}}{=}0.30$, $\tau_q^{\text{small}}{=}0.15$. 
A bypass threshold $\tau_{\text{skip}}$ ($0.70$ for large classes, $0.40$ for small) is recorded per mask during inference; whether $\alpha \geq \tau_{\text{skip}}$ is stored in the result JSON so that the accuracy–efficiency trade-off of skipping the BBox agent can be evaluated entirely offline from saved outputs.

\paragraph{Failure‑Mode Agent}
Classifies the dominant error type (\texttt{boundary\_drift}, \texttt{hallucination}, \texttt{occlusion\_miss}, \texttt{fragmentation}). 
This agent is diagnostic only and does not affect triage.

\paragraph{Correction Agent}
Determines whether a mask can be improved and proposes a single refinement step (\texttt{refine} or \texttt{no\_refine}).

\paragraph{Consistency Check}
A deterministic LiDAR‑support test: the fraction of mask pixels with projected LiDAR returns must exceed $\tau{=}0.1$. 
This check reliably detects masks leaking into sky or void regions.

All VLM agents return constrained categorical outputs to ensure reproducibility.

\subsection{Swin‑Based Object Discovery}

Triage removes false positives but cannot detect objects SAM missed. 
We therefore introduce a discovery step that compares the Swin class map to the SAM annotation at $384{\times}384$ resolution. 
Pixels where Swin predicts a non‑background class but SAM predicts background form candidate regions. 
Connected components below a minimum size are discarded; the largest remaining components (up to $K_{\text{disc}}$ per frame) are cropped and verified by a VLM. 
For vehicles and signs, the VLM confirms presence; for humans, it also disambiguates cyclist vs.\ pedestrian. 
Confirmed regions are added as new masks, modifying only pixels still labeled as background.

\subsection{Risk‑Aware Deterministic Triage}

Instead of weighted scoring, we use a deterministic rule requiring concordant evidence for rejection. 
Rejection is destructive—deleted pixels cannot be recovered—so relying on any single negative signal would compound false positives across agents. 
Let $\mathcal{N}=\{\texttt{invalid},\texttt{bad},\texttt{fail}\}$ denote negative signals.

A mask is \textbf{rejected} if at least two signals in $\mathcal{N}$ are present. 
A \texttt{background} verdict counts as two signals and rejects immediately.

A mask is \textbf{accepted} if the BBox agent outputs \texttt{valid}, the quality agent outputs \texttt{good}, and the consistency check outputs \texttt{pass}.

A mask is routed to \textbf{refinement} if it is valid and of good quality but fails the consistency check and the correction agent recommends refinement.

All remaining cases—exactly one dissenting judge—are sent to \textbf{human review}. 
Review masks are retained but flagged, ensuring that no single erroneous judgment silently deletes valid labels.

\subsection{Interpretability and Reproducibility}

All prompts, thresholds, and decision rules are fixed before evaluation. 
Every triage outcome can be traced to explicit agent outputs, and no learned fusion weights or continuous scores are used, ensuring reproducibility across datasets and models.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work introduced a modular multi-agent framework for validating, refining, and augmenting SAM-based semantic segmentation annotations in autonomous driving. 
By combining object-scale multimodal crops with specialized visual–LLM agents and a domain-specific Swin Transformer, the system provides interpretable, risk-aware triage in which destructive rejection requires agreement between independent judges. 
This prevents false-positive decisions from compounding while preserving transparency and reproducibility.

A key insight is that a dataset-adapted segmentation model offers a stronger and more stable quality signal than a general-purpose VLM, while the VLM remains essential for semantic tasks such as confirming object identity, detecting background surfaces, and disambiguating human subclasses during discovery. 
Class-aware thresholds further ensure that small-object masks are not over-rejected due to lower spatial resolution.

Beyond filtering erroneous masks, the Swin-based discovery module recovers objects missed by SAM, enabling a bidirectional correction process that addresses both false positives and false negatives. 
The clean/flagged frame partition from our earlier annotation campaign provides large-scale weak supervision and supports downstream experiments in which filtered versions of discarded frames are returned to the training pool.

The architecture is backend-agnostic and supports local deployment, making it suitable for privacy-sensitive workflows. 
Future work includes evaluation on a second automotive dataset (ISEAuto) to test generalization across sensor configurations, adaptive prompting, multi-agent debate mechanisms, and extensions to panoptic and temporal segmentation. 
Overall, the framework offers a reproducible foundation for scalable annotation quality control in autonomous-driving datasets.
